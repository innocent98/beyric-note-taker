# SOP: billing stage S2, enforce the hours rule per person, with the new warnings

**What shipped.** With `HOURS_MODE=enforce` (still `off` by default; Adebayo switches it on himself) the person's own grants decide whether a new recording starts, each counted recording is split across their grants, and the person gets an 80% notice and a 100% notice (a bell entry and one mail each, once per person and period). The refusal says `reason` (`no_hours` or `no_plan`) and no longer says "pilot" or "hello@". `usage` in the session payload carries new fields. With `off` or `shadow` nothing changes for any person's joins. PR: #214, branch `feature/hours-enforce`. It is stage S2 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-billing-and-plans-design.md` (section 8, plus 2.2, 3.1 to 3.3 and 3.7). No migration. FE guide: `docs/fe-integration-guide-billing.md` (captures in `docs/fe-captures/hours-usage/`).

## Why

S1 put a person's hours into grants and compared the new rule with the old cap in a log. S2 makes the new rule the one that decides, so a plan, a trial, a pack and an admin gift can each be spent in a fixed order, a grant's hours left is always a sum (auditable), and the person is told once when their hours run low or out.

## How, and what was decided

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| The block in `reserve_slot` (`app/placement.py`) | `enforce`: `entitlements.check_join` decides, under the placement lock, and raises 403 `hours_cap_reached` with `reason` (`no_hours`: hours held, all used; `no_plan`: none held). `off` and `shadow`: the old cap rule decides as before (shadow still logs). A running meeting is never touched: the rule only decides whether a NEW recording starts |
| Spending order (decided by the lead, 2026-11-16; spec 3.2 has the line) | (1) trial or pilot, (2) plan hours, oldest period first, (3) admin gifts and packs together, earliest `expires_at` first, no expiry last. The rule: whatever ends soonest is used first. `entitlements.spend_key` is the one place |
| State | a person with a plan period in force is `active`, even while a pilot grant still has hours (S1 review: it used to follow the spending order and showed `pilot`) and even with the plan's hours spent. Otherwise the first trial or pilot grant with hours; a pack or a gift alone is `none`. The admin is `unlimited` and is checked first everywhere |
| Allocation (`app/hours.py`, `allocate`) | each counted recording is split across the person's grants in force at the count, taking from the first grant in order and spilling into the next, into `usage_allocations` (a recording can have two rows). Idempotent per entry. An admin gets none. A **per-person advisory lock** (`HOURS_LOCK` 6020) stops two workers counting two recordings of one person at the same moment from spending the same hour twice |
| A recording longer than what is left | counted **in full** (a running meeting finishes), the excess **written off** (not billed, not carried into the next period; spec 3.3). It is stored as an over-draw on the last grant spent from (a row larger than that grant had left, which `entitlements` reads as zero left, never less). That row also marks the entry settled. Decided: this keeps "an entry with no allocation" meaning exactly "usage counted before allocations existed" |
| When allocations are written | **`enforce` only** (and by the reconcile script). `off` and `shadow` write nothing and take no lock: shadow only logs. Under `enforce` the allocation and the notices are **best effort**, each in its own savepoint: a failure is logged (`hours allocation failed user=.. entry=.. error=<Class>` or `hours notice failed ...`, no message because it could carry parameters) and the transcript goes on, so a paying person's meeting is never failed by bookkeeping. The recording stays counted in the ledger. It is **not** retried by itself: until `scripts/hours_reconcile_backlog.py` runs, a person who holds a pilot grant is still charged for it by the backlog term; a person with no pilot grant (a plan only, from S8) would not be, so grep the log for `allocation failed` and decide before S8 whether a sweep is needed |
| Usage counted during S1, and cap raises made before `enforce` (decided: how the first enforce run treats them) | Stage S1 wrote no allocations; `entitlements.unallocated_seconds` took that usage off the grants anyway. `scripts/hours_reconcile_backlog.py` (dry run by default, `--apply` keeps it) does two things per person. **(1) Cap gifts:** it gives the difference between the person's cap and the cap their grants were built from (pilot grants plus the ledger when they were made; the app default for an account with no pilot grant) as `admin_gift` grants, derived from state so a cap lowered and raised again is gifted once (`hours.sync_cap_gift`); this is the only step that adds hours and it is shown as "gift". **(2) The S1 backlog:** it turns the S1-window usage into allocations on the person's pilot grants, so it can never come off paid hours when a pilot grant later expires (S10). Normally this changes nobody's hours left. It **can**: when the backlog is larger than what the pilot grant had left and the person holds other grants, the excess was coming off those other grants and is now written off (example: pilot 1 h, 3 h used in S1, a 5 h gift: 3 h left before, 5 h after). The script prints hours left before and after settling for every person, marks a change `<-- HOURS CHANGE`, and **refuses `--apply` while any person's hours would change** (exit 2, nothing kept), so the lead sees it first. The usage term is bounded by the pilot grant's expiry (never, if any pilot grant of the person has none), never counts an entry that has any allocation (a written-off shortfall included), and excludes the recording being allocated |
| The notices (spec 3.3) | 80%: used reaches 80% of what the person holds (every grant in force, spent or not; integer math, so 79.9% does not warn and 80% does). 100%: nothing left. Both are checked when a recording is counted, so a jump from 50% to 100% sends only the 100% notice. Dedupe key = kind + the person's **period key** (the newest plan period in force, else the first grant in spending order, else `none`), so one per person and period. The same 100% key is used when a calendar meeting is skipped for want of hours, so many skipped meetings raise one notice, not one each. Mail goes through `alerts.notify_user` (respects `notify_email`, bounces and suppression). The old once-ever 80% notice (`usage.hours_low` key `usage.hours_low`) runs only in `off` and `shadow` |
| Join runner | under `enforce` a skipped meeting gets skip reason `hours_cap` (`no_hours`) or the new `no_plan` (none held); both are retried until the deadline like `hours_cap` was, and both list as "Not recorded" with Send now. `no_plan` was added to `RETRYABLE_SKIPS`, `MOVABLE_SKIPS`, `NOT_RECORDED_SKIP_REASONS` and the reason texts. The per-meeting `calendar.hours_cap` notice stays in `off` and `shadow` |
| Refusal words | `hours_cap_message` (`app/services.py`) no longer names a pilot or an address. Three texts: the old cap rule names the cap, `no_hours`, `no_plan`. This text changes in every mode (the old "pilot" wording is wrong whatever the flag says); the decision does not |
| Admin cap edit (S1 review follow-up) | Under `enforce`, `admin.update_user` calls `hours.sync_cap_gift`: the cap gifts (reason "Recorded-hours cap raised by an admin", no expiry) always add up to `cap - baseline`, never stacking: 5 h to 10 h to 3 h to 10 h gives 5 h once (tested). Lowering takes nothing back. **Under `off` and `shadow` a cap edit changes the cap and nothing else**, as before; edits made then are gifted by the reconcile script |
| `usage` in the session payload | old fields kept; under `enforce` they are read from the grants (`cap_seconds` = hours held now, `used_seconds` = held minus left, running meetings counted). New fields `state`, `seconds_left`, `warning`, `can_record`, `unlimited`, `period_ends_at`, `trial_ends_at`; **all null unless `enforce`** |

Rejected: writing allocations in `off` or `shadow` (the lead decided shadow only logs; an earlier draft wrote them in shadow, under a lock); a gift computed from the edit (it stacked); a migration for a "settled" marker (it would have clashed with the billing provider layer's 0050 and the over-draw row does the same job); deciding the 80% share against the old lifetime cap for pilot accounts (the spec says "of what the person holds").

## Before switching on (for Adebayo; none of this is done by the deploy)

1. **Accounts made after migration 0049 hold no hours** (the trial is stage S10). Under `enforce` they are refused with `no_plan`, and so is any new sign-up while sign-up is open. Close that first: the S10 trial, or a grant for those accounts. `scripts/hours_shadow_report.py` lists them as `old rule allows, new rule refuses: no grant in force`.
2. **MUST DECIDE BEFORE ENFORCE: one person can start two meetings on their last minute.** A meeting just placed counts as about 0 s (`usage.running_seconds` measures from `started_polling_at`, set at send time), so with 60 s left a person can paste two links 20 s apart and `check_join` allows both. Each is then counted in full and the excess is written off; at the bot limit of 2 and up to `max_poll_hours` (6 h) each, up to about 12 h per person and period go unbilled (at 0.56 to 0.66 USD an hour, about 6.7 to 7.9 USD of our cost per occurrence). Spec 2.3 said the placement lock prevents this; it only puts joins in order (corrected there). Options, **not chosen, the owner decides**:
   * **A. Reserve a floor per running or just-placed meeting** (count each at `max(elapsed, N minutes)`, for example 15 or 30). One-line change in how running meetings are estimated under `enforce`. Cost: a person with less than N minutes per meeting is refused although they have time (with 2 meetings and N=30, 1 h must be free to start the second); the floor also shows up in `seconds_left` while a meeting runs, and a short meeting "gives back" the difference at the count.
   * **B. Refuse a second concurrent recording when hours left are below a minimum M** (for example 1 h), the first one unaffected. Cost: people near zero can run one meeting at a time only; the second is refused with the usual 403 and a Send now later. Simple, the leak is bounded by M per extra meeting; needs a decision on M and a new reason or message.
   * **C. Hold hours at placement and release them at the count** (a hold row per placed meeting, taken under the placement lock, released when the recording is counted or the meeting fails). Exact, no over-refusal. Cost: a migration and every failure path must release the hold (a leaked hold locks hours), the most code and the most risk.
   * **D. Accept it.** Worst case about 12 h per person per period of our cost (about 6.7 to 7.9 USD), only for a person who does this on purpose, and only on their last minute; the written-off excess is already the decided overshoot rule for one meeting. Cost: nothing to build; the 2-meeting limit bounds it.
3. **Pilot people's numbers reset when the flag flips.** Under `enforce`, `cap_seconds` and `used_seconds` are read from the grant they hold (the cap minus what was used at migration), not the old 5 hours: a person who had used 4 h of 5 sees "0 of 1 h", and the 80% mail says "of the 1 recorded hours you have now". The FE guide lists it as a UX consequence. Open question for the lead and the owner: should the screens instead show the original pilot total (5 h)? That needs the migration's baseline kept next to the grant.
4. **The share page** (`web/app/share/page.tsx`) is fixed in this PR. After the deploy, open a shared link as an account with no hours and read it: it must say the plain words, not a pilot or an address. The only other places that name the pilot are the Terms and the Privacy Policy (S11); `web/components/share/share-flow.tsx` only shows the message it is given.
5. Deploy S2 with `HOURS_MODE` unset (nothing changes), then `shadow` (logging only; it writes nothing), recreate web, worker and scheduler.
6. `docker compose exec -T web python scripts/hours_reconcile_backlog.py </dev/null` (a dry run: per person "user 1", "user 2": the cap gift, hours settled, hours left before and after settling). **Read every line marked `HOURS CHANGE`: `--apply` refuses while there is one.** Then the same with `--apply`. Running it again changes nothing.
7. `docker compose exec -T web python scripts/hours_shadow_report.py </dev/null`: every disagreement must be explained (point 1 is the expected one).
8. Set `HOURS_MODE=enforce`, recreate web, worker and scheduler.

## Operate / roll back

Roll back the behaviour by setting `off` (or `shadow`) and recreating. The grants, allocations and gifts stay and do nothing; the old rule reads the ledger, which `enforce` kept up to date (the same counts), so the two rules agree again at once. Notices already sent stay. Revert the PR to remove the code; there is no migration to undo.
