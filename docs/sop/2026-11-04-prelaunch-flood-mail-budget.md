# SOP: before sign-up opens, part 2: flood defence, mail budget, counters in the database

## What shipped
Three items from the sign-up SOP's "fix before sign-up is switched on" list: (1) the login and sign-up counters moved from memory to the database (migration `0016`), so a restart does not end a lockout and a second replica shares them; (2) a better sign-up flood defence (a per-address cap on unconfirmed accounts, clearing the busiest address's rows first, never a quiet one's); (3) a global per-hour budget for all outgoing mail with a reserve for sign-in mail, held mail waiting with a reason and the admins told. Branch `feature/prelaunch-flood-mail-budget`, one PR (number in the PR list). No new route and no response shape changed.

## Why
The sign-up SOP recorded that "about 20 addresses x 5 an hour keep the 100-unconfirmed cap full and everyone gets 429; the at-cap clearing can evict a real person's pending account after an hour; no global cap on outgoing mail", and that "limits are in memory and per process". With password sign-up about to open, each of those could turn a nuisance into an outage (nobody can sign up, nobody gets a verification or reset mail, Resend's daily quota is gone).

## How
| Piece | Decision |
|---|---|
| Counters in the database | `app/ratelimit.py`: a `Store` with two implementations behind one interface. **`DbStore` is the app's**: table `rate_hits (bucket, key, at)`; `acquire(gates)` is one transaction under `pg_advisory_xact_lock(7001, hashtext(bucket:key))` for every key involved, **taken in sorted order** (two calls that share keys cannot deadlock), counts each gate, answers the first full gate's label and otherwise inserts a hit on every gate (a refused attempt costs nothing, an address over its limit does not spend an account's budget). `MemoryStore` has the same behaviour for unit tests. `LoginLimiter` (`app/auth.py`) and `limits.Limit` (`app/limits.py`) keep their methods and now sit on a store; the old in-process `_Window` moved into `ratelimit.py` |
| What is stored | The bucket's name, a **keyed hash** of the key (HMAC with a key derived from the session secret, 32 hex characters: an email address or a client address is personal data, and a reader of the table cannot check a guess) and the time as epoch seconds. Rows older than their window are deleted when the key is touched; the hourly sweep (`maintenance.sweep_rate_hits`) deletes anything over 49 hours old (the per-address count lives 48) |
| Time | Epoch seconds (`time.time()`) instead of the process's monotonic clock, because the hits outlive the process |
| Wiring | `create_app` builds one `DbStore` whose session comes from `get_session` or whatever overrides it (`main._own_session`: the limiter has a small connection pool of its own in the app, `app.db.get_rate_session`, so a request that holds a connection and counts a mail inside its transaction cannot starve it; tests use their override); a count commits at once in a session of its own, apart from the request's transaction. A database failure raises (login needs the database anyway; failing open would let a guessing attack through during an outage) |
| Per-address cap | `limits.SIGNUP_ORIGIN_CAP = 3` sign-ups per client address in 48 hours (the life of an unconfirmed account), counted in the database (`signup_origin` bucket) for **every submission that gets as far as the pilot checks, whether the email is new or already has an account**; the fourth is 429 `too_many_requests`, before the lookup. **Why a hit counter and not a count of rows** (found by the automated review): counting the rows an address holds differs between a new email (a row appears) and one that already has an account (none does), so after two sign-ups an attacker could submit a third email and learn from the next refusal whether it was new; a test runs both worlds and asserts the same answers. Confirming an account gives its hit back (`signup.verify_email`), so a person who confirmed can sign up again; the cost is that a person who re-submits to fix a typo uses a hit, and one address (an office) has three sign-ups a day or two. `users.signup_origin` is a keyed hash of the client address (`ratelimit.origin_key`, its own label), set when the row is created and **never changed by a later submission** (otherwise an attacker could re-submit a victim's address from the address being flooded to make the victim's row look like part of the flood); it is what the clearing below groups by. Rows from before the column have none and are each their own address |
| Clearing at the global cap | At `MAX_UNVERIFIED = 100` the old rule deleted every unconfirmed row older than an hour, which pushed out a real person's row while an attacker's fresh rows stayed. **Now one row at a time is deleted, chosen by address**: the oldest row of the address that holds the most unconfirmed rows, as long as it holds at least two. When every address holds one row nobody can tell an attacker's from a real person's, so only a row **over an hour old** is cleared (oldest first) and a fresh one never is; if there is none the newcomer is refused (429). Invited people's rows are never candidates. Under the sign-up lock as before |
| Mail budget | `MAIL_HOURLY_BUDGET` (default 30, 0 = off) counts mails **sent in the last rolling hour, of every kind** (as Resend counts them). **Password-reset and invitation mail may use all of it**; verification mail and "already registered" mail (anyone can make the site send them by filling in the sign-up form), notes mail and later share and reminder mail may **not use the last `MAIL_HOURLY_RESERVE`** (default 10). So a flood of sign-ups, which fills the verification share, cannot keep a real person from a reset link or an invitation (found by the automated review: the first version let verification mail use the whole budget). Admin alerts (`alert` template) are **never held** and go first, so the admins can still be told. Over budget a mail stays `pending`, no attempt is used, `last_error = over_budget`, and it goes when the hour rolls on; a sign-in link that waits more than an hour is failed by the sweep **keeping the reason** (`over_budget`, listed by `GET /api/admin/mail-failures`), a notes mail after its 24-hour hold likewise. The count and the send are done under one advisory lock (`7002, 1`) held to the commit, so two workers cannot each see room and both send past the budget (a test with a slow mailer fails without the lock) |
| Telling the admins | When a pass holds mail, `mail._report_budget` logs the counts (once in ten minutes, never an address) and calls `alerts.notify_admins` (`admin.mail_budget`, one notice per clock hour by dedupe key, counts only, no address). It never raises: the queue goes on |
| Rejected | A captcha or proof of work (changes the designed form and needs a third party or a client script); one global hourly limit on sign-up submissions (it hands the attacker the outage); evicting by age alone (the reported problem); a shared in-memory store behind Redis (a new service for a pilot) |

## Changes after the Opus review (2026-11-04)
| Finding | What changed |
|---|---|
| **H1** The reserve was defeated once more than 100 held mails were pending: `pending_ids` loaded the oldest 100, held rows stay pending, so a reset link queued behind them was never looked at | `deliver_pending` loads in three queries so one kind cannot hide another: admin alerts and protected mail (reset, invitation) on their own first, then other account mail, then notes mail. Of the other account mail, rows never held come before held ones, so a real person's new verification mail is not stuck behind the backlog. Tests: 130 held verification mails queued ahead of a reset link, an invitation and an alert (each still goes), and a new verification mail going before the held backlog |
| **M1** A reset flood could use the whole budget and starve invitations and other people's resets | The cause is the request, not the mail: `forgot_email_hour` allows 3 reset requests an hour per email (besides 1 a minute and 5 an hour per address); the rest answer the same 202 and do nothing, so no new token revokes the earlier link and no mail is queued. A cap at send time would have been worse (the 4th request would revoke the third link and send nothing). Test: 6 requests, 3 mails; the cap is per email |
| **M2** A lock on an account (10 failures an hour from 2+ addresses) outlived a restart and nothing cleared it | A successful password reset clears that account's counter (`LoginLimiter.unlock`, called by `signup.reset_password`; only that account). `scripts/unlock_login.py <email>` does the same for the operator (the rows are keyed hashes, so they cannot be found by hand). Tests: the lock, a reset from another address, a login from a third new address at once; another account stays locked; the script clears only that account and says how many hits it cleared. Live: `docs/fe-captures/prelaunch-flood-defence/` 20 to 28 |
| **M3** Without `EDGE_SHARED_SECRET` every visitor shares one address, so three sign-ups would close the form for 48 hours | In production with **no secret configured at all** a request has no origin: the per-address cap is skipped and one warning a day says why. When a secret IS configured, a request without it did not come through nginx (anything on the host can reach `web` and `next` on localhost) and **keeps the cap on the address it really has** (round-two review: skipping it for such requests would have let them past the 48-hour cap and made their rows resist eviction). The 5-an-hour limits are unchanged. Tests: no secret configured, a wrong secret (capped, rows carry an origin), the right secret (the cap applies to the forwarded address) |
| **L1, L2** The sweep kept every bucket 49 hours; its test could not fail | `sweep_rate_hits` keeps `signup_origin` hits 49 hours and everything else 2 hours. The test seeds hits of each bucket and age and checks what is left. The unused `DbStore.sweep` is removed |
| **L7** The budget exemption was keyed on the `alert` template | Keyed on the recipient: an alert to an active admin or to `ADMIN_ALERT_EMAIL` skips the budget; an alert to anyone else is held like other mail. (The queue order still puts alert templates first: harmless, because only the exemption can spend past the budget.) |
| **L9** `MAIL_HOURLY_BUDGET=0` means unlimited | Kept (it is how the other caps read), and said plainly in `.env.example`, `deploy/README.md` and the config comment: 0 is no budget, it does not stop mail |
| **L10** An evicted account's pending verification mail still sent | `accounts.delete_unverified` also deletes that address's still-pending verification mail (the sweep and the eviction both use it) |
| Two notes from the automated security review of the review fixes (acknowledged, not changed) | **Reset cap:** someone who knows an address can use up its 3 reset requests an hour, so the owner's own 4th request that hour is swallowed; but the last link is mailed to the OWNER's inbox and stays valid 30 minutes, and before this change the same person could revoke the owner's link every minute without end, so the window is bounded now (at most about 30 minutes an hour), not new. Not re-sending an unexpired link is impossible (only its hash is stored). **No origin with no secret configured:** skipping the cap there is the safe side: with no secret every visitor shares the proxy's address, and treating their rows as one shared "address" for eviction would label every real person as one flooder; the 5-an-hour limits still hold |
| Left, with reasons | **L3** confirming an invited account gives its address a hit it never spent (invitations are admin-issued; the worst case is one extra sign-up for that address). **L4** confirming bypasses the cap: bounded by `max_accounts` (409 `pilot_full`). **L5** a shared office address gets 3 sign-ups per 48 hours (invitations bypass it; the guide words the message). **L6** IPv6 is grouped by /64 (`edge.limiter_key`); a /48 customer has 65,536 of them: a wider group for this one bucket is possible, but changes the address model for every limit, so it waits for a real case. **L8** the budget lock is held across the provider call (up to 15 s): by design, it only serialises workers; `deliver_pending` must not be called from `web`. **L11** the timing of a new versus an existing email predates this change (argon2 dominates) |

## Last polish after the second Opus review (2026-11-04: merge-ready, no High or Medium)
| Finding | What changed |
|---|---|
| **L-R3** a request that skips nginx skipped the 48-hour cap too | Fixed as in the M3 row above |
| **L-R4** a held backlog cost up to 300 rows x (row lock, suppression check, the global lock, a count) every 10 s | Once one mail has been held in a pass, every later verification or notes mail is held without a query and all of them are marked `over_budget` in one statement at the end (privileged mail is always loaded first and still checked row by row). Test: a pass over 55 held rows asks `_sent_since` at most 3 times and marks all 55; a fairness test shows a real person's new mail still goes first after a skipped pass |
| **L-R6** `unlock` failing after the password commit gave a 500 | It is wrapped: the failure is logged by exception type only and the answer is 200 (the password did change). Test: a broken `unlock` still gives 200 and the new password logs in |
| **L-T1** the alert test passed without the H1 fix | Renamed for what it guards (ordering), and a new test queues 130 held alerts to non-admins and then an admin alert: the privileged load now also puts never-held rows first, and the test fails without that (checked) |
| **L-T2** the unlock script's real path was not run | A test points the script's `get_settings` and `SessionLocal` at the test database and runs `run([email])`: the key matches the app's, the lock ends, a second run says nothing was recorded |
| **L9** `MAIL_HOURLY_BUDGET <= 0` is unlimited | Documented (config, `.env.example`, README) and `mail.warn_if_unlimited` logs one warning at start (once a day at most) that the budget is off and that 0 does not stop mail |
| Accepted, named | **L-R1** an attacker who knows the owner's email can leave the owner without a usable reset link for up to about 28 minutes in each hour (the newest mailed link works for 30 minutes; a known device, a past login and the unlock script are other ways in); the frontend says "if no email arrives, use the newest one we sent; links last 30 minutes". **L-R2** about ten known confirmed emails x 3 resets an hour can fill the whole budget (reset mail has reserve 0), so invitations and other people's resets wait and, past an hour, are dropped as `over_budget`; and the earlier link of those users was already revoked when the new one was issued, so this is the one path where a link is made and never sent although the person was told to check their email. Not fixed (the revocation happens at request time, the hold later); the admins get the hourly notice. Pilot scale: the emails are not public. **L-R5** notes mail is tried last, so a sustained verification flood starves it until it fails as `over_budget` after 24 hours (the notes are in the app anyway). **L-R7** deleting an evicted account's pending mail may wait for a row the worker is sending (up to the 15-second provider timeout), under the sign-up lock. **L-R8** the raw token of a capture stays in an old commit of the branch (spent, expired, from a scratch database) |

## Operate / roll back
- **Server steps before merge: none required.** `MAIL_HOURLY_BUDGET` (30) and `MAIL_HOURLY_RESERVE` (10) are optional with safe defaults (`.env.example`, `deploy/README.md`). On Resend's Pro plan raise the budget or set 0. Migration `0016` runs on deploy (additive).
- **`EDGE_SHARED_SECRET` must be set** (it already is in production; nginx sends it). Without it the per-address sign-up cap is skipped and the log says so once a day (see the review table).
- **A locked account** (the owner locked out by guesses from other addresses): a password reset ends it by itself; or, on the server, `docker compose exec web python scripts/unlock_login.py someone@example.com` (clears that account's failed-login counter only).
- After deploy the first login lockouts and sign-up counts start from zero (nothing is carried over from memory).
- Mail held by the budget: its `mail_outbox` row says `pending` with `last_error = over_budget`; after it expires, `failed` with the same reason in `GET /api/admin/mail-failures`. Admins get a bell entry (and an alert mail) `admin.mail_budget` at most once an hour.
- Roll back: revert the PR. `alembic downgrade 0015` drops `rate_hits` and `users.signup_origin` (the counters restart empty; no user data is lost). The old image ignores both.

## Follow-ups
- **A flood from about 100 distinct addresses still fills the unconfirmed table** (nothing can tell their rows from real people's); then fresh sign-ups are refused until rows age past an hour or are confirmed. A captcha or proof of work is the next step if that is ever seen; the mail budget bounds what such a flood can cost.
- A person behind a shared address can hit the per-address cap of 3 although they did nothing (the guide says how the frontend words it).
- Account mail still has no daily cap of its own (only the hourly budget); on Resend's Free plan 100 a day is below 30 an hour for four hours.
- The global cap at 100 unconfirmed accounts still answers by a count of rows, so someone who can bring the count to exactly 99 could see one more sign-up refused; it needs the whole table to be near full and was already so before this change.
- A forgot-password flood against known confirmed addresses (pilot size: 10) could use the reset share of the budget; it is limited per address and per email already (5 an hour, 1 a minute).
- The per-email 1-a-minute and per-address hourly limits for resend, forgot and waitlist are unchanged, now in the database.
- Resend, forgot-password and waitlist rows are not capped per address like sign-up (they create no accounts).
