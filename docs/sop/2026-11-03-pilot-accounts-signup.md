# SOP: pilot accounts, sign-up (Phase A, tasks A3 and A10 to A20)

## What shipped
The second half of Phase A: sign-up with a password or with Google, email verification, password reset, the waitlist and invitations, the admin API, deleting an account for good, the notifications API, the mailer, nginx limits for the public routes, and the privacy and terms wording for accounts. Branch `feature/pilot-accounts-signup` (PR: see the PR list), one PR (about 2,860 changed lines of app and deploy code, plus a 956-line capture script; tests, captures and generated files not counted). Also from the Opus review of the accounts core: `bot_already_active` is per user with a neutral `bot_busy` for a full note taker, usage is committed before transcription, and the Jinja IDOR tests exist.

**Sign-up is still closed**: `app_state.signups_open` stays `false`. Nothing here opens it, nothing here is deployed. The pages the emailed links open (`/verify-email`, `/reset-password`, `/signup?invite=`, `/pilot-full`, `/login?error=`) are frontend work (stream D) and must exist before the admin opens the pilot.

## Changes after the Opus review of this PR (2026-11-03)
| Finding | What changed |
|---|---|
| Deleting a user with a live meeting could remove the row while Vexa kept the audio | `app/deletion.py` asks Vexa by the meeting id when no recording id is stored, and waits (retries next pass) unless Vexa says the meeting `failed`, or `completed` with no recording once the pipeline's 10-minute grace has passed; a recording that exists without a final id is waited for too. Any exception from one user's purge, the mail job or the purge job is caught per item and logged by type only (`worker.background_jobs`), so it can no longer stall every user's meetings |
| Race: a re-submission could overwrite the password of an account confirmed a moment earlier | The sign-up (and the Google link step) lock the account row (`FOR UPDATE`); `verify_email` takes the sign-up lock and then the row before writing and looks again. A test widens the window and fails without the lock |
| Sign-up plus login was an enumeration oracle (403 `email_not_verified` vs 401) | An unconfirmed account now answers like a wrong password (401 `invalid_credentials`, same hash cost); `email_not_verified` no longer exists. The "confirm your email" screen belongs after sign-up |
| Unconfirmed accounts held pilot places for 48 h | **Chosen: confirmed accounts, unconfirmed accounts made from an invitation, and live invitations hold places; a plain unconfirmed account holds none.** Confirmation re-checks room under the sign-up lock (409 `pilot_full`, the link survives); Google linking checks too; at most 100 unconfirmed password accounts exist at once and rows older than an hour are cleared before anyone is refused (429 for all alike); the sweep counts from the last sign-up submission (a resend does not extend it) and returns an invited person to the waiting list. Spec section 4.4 changed accordingly. Rejected: a 2-hour hold (still fills the pilot from a few addresses) |
| Raw tokens in `mail_outbox.payload` contradicted "only hashes" | **The raw link lives in the outbox until the mail is sent** (cannot be avoided without encrypting the payload, which needs the Phase C key). Exposure cut: with no mail provider no token or row is made at all (password sign-up off, forgot and resend answer 202 and do nothing, the admin invite returns the link), unsent mail is failed and scrubbed once it is over an hour old (was 24 hours; the hourly sweep does it, so a row can wait up to about **two hours**), scrubbed at send and at final failure. Spec, SOP and `tokens.py` now say where a raw token lives and that backups taken in that window can hold a live link |
| Admin reset link | Accepted for the pilot and stated plainly: **it lets the administrator sign in as that user**. It is refused for admin accounts and for unconfirmed or suspended ones, logged with ids only, the user gets a `warning` notification, and the user is signed out everywhere **when the link is used** (the reset bumps their epoch), not when it is made. The privacy policy says so ("Security") |
| HTTP clients never closed | The Google exchange opens and closes a client per call; asking whether mail is configured (`mail.is_configured`) builds no client |
| Resend missing from the privacy policy | Added to "Who we share information with" and the transfers paragraph, Jinja and Next copies |

## Changes after the second Opus review (2026-11-03)
| Finding | What changed |
|---|---|
| The worker kept processing a deleted user's meeting while the purge waited for Vexa | `worker.next_meeting_id` skips meetings of deleted owners and `pipeline.advance_meeting` looks at the owner before every step, so nothing is downloaded, sent to OpenAI, written or counted for them; a test deletes the account mid-run (between two steps) and one through the admin API mid-meeting that ends in a full purge with no OpenAI call |
| The purge could stall for ever | "Settled" is defined (module docstring of `app/deletion.py`): Vexa does not know the meeting (404, logged at error level with the meeting id when no recording id is stored), or the bot is gone and a recording with audio is deleted, or the meeting or recording `failed`, or no audio appeared within the pipeline's 10-minute grace counted from when **the purge** first saw the meeting over (stamped on the row, never from creation). A recording still `in_progress` keeps waiting. A bot is stopped whatever our own status says (a meeting given up after 6 h is `failed` here while its bot may still be in the call). **After 24 hours (`users.deleted_at`, migration 0008) the purge gives up:** it logs an error naming the meeting ids, notifies every admin in the app (`admin.purge_forced`, ids only, never the address) and deletes our rows without calling Vexa again, so the Vexa copy of those meetings is the one thing that can outlive the promise, and the admin is told which. `deleted_at` is set by the admin API; the first failed pass sets it for an account marked by hand. `VexaNotFound` is a new subclass of `VexaError` (a 404 on the meeting lookup) |
| An invitation could not help someone who already had an unconfirmed account | The person's own live invitation is not counted against them when they confirm; confirming on it, signing up again with `invite_token`, or linking Google redeems it (`signup.redeem_invite`: the invite is used up and the waitlist entry becomes `joined`, so the place is counted once, through the entry). Tests for password sign-up, plain confirmation and Google |
| Confirmation could deadlock with sign-up or the Google link | `verify_email` takes the sign-up lock **before** consuming the token (the order everywhere is: sign-up lock, token row, account row). A threaded test widens the window and fails without the order |
| Resend named too narrowly in the privacy policy | It now lists verification, resets, waiting-list invitations and "already registered" notices, and what Resend receives (Jinja and Next copies) |
| An hourly job that always failed stopped the others | `worker.hourly_jobs` and `maintenance.run_hourly` isolate each job (audio cleanup; stale rows, unverified sweep, mail outbox scrub), logged by type only |
| `delete_user.py` checked for the last admin without the lock | It takes `ADMIN_LOCK` after its question (never while a person types) and checks again before purging (`admin.last_admin_locked`) |
| The scrub window was described as one hour | Corrected to up to about two hours (the hourly sweep) in `mail.py`, `tokens.py`, the spec and here. The admin reset link signs the user out **when it is used**, not when it is made |

## Why
Spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-pilot-accounts-calendar-design.md` sections 4, 5, 6, 12, 13 and task list 15. Ten testers must be able to create accounts without the owner doing it by hand, without sign-up becoming a way to learn who is registered, and without a second user being able to see or block the first.

## How
| Piece | Decision |
|---|---|
| Mail (`app/mail.py`) | `Mailer` with `ResendMailer` (one POST with an `Idempotency-Key`, errors kept by kind only), `ConsoleMailer` (development; **refused in production**, also at start). No key in production = no mailer: nothing is queued at all (see the review table). Actions only `enqueue` a row in their own transaction, so a mail provider can never fail an action. The worker sends: tries at 0, 1, 6 and 36 minutes after the mail was queued (so retries 1, 5 and 30 minutes after the try before; counted from `created_at`, no new column). A sent or finally failed row has its link `payload` replaced by `{}`; unsent rows are failed and scrubbed once over an hour old (by the hourly sweep: up to about two hours); sent rows deleted after 30 days. A name goes into a greeting only if it is plainly letters, spaces, apostrophes and hyphens (up to 60), otherwise "Hello," (the waitlist form takes a name from anyone) |
| Tokens (`app/tokens.py`) | 32 random bytes, only the SHA-256 stored in `auth_tokens` (the raw link also sits in `mail_outbox.payload` until sent: see the table above), single use (`consume` is one `UPDATE ... RETURNING`), purposes `verify_email` 24 h, `reset_password` 30 min, `waitlist_invite` 7 days |
| Sign-up (`app/signup.py`) | Always 202 `check_your_email`, for a new address, an existing one, and a repeat. Validation (email, name, terms, password) and the pilot's state (`pilot_closed`, `pilot_full`) are checked first and are the same for everybody. One argon2 hash is always computed, so an existing address costs the same time. Places are counted under the advisory lock `SIGNUP_LOCK = 6002`: confirmed non-admin users (suspended included), unconfirmed accounts made from an invitation, and live invitations. A place is taken when an account is **confirmed**, so ten parallel confirmations at nine accounts confirm one (the test widens the gap so only the lock decides, and fails without it); ten parallel new Google sign-ups at nine accounts also make one. The terms version accepted (`TERMS_VERSION`, the "Last updated" date of the legal pages, a test keeps them equal) is stored on the account |
| Pre-registration defence | **Each verification token carries the hash of the password from its own sign-up (`auth_tokens.pending_password_hash`, migration 0008) and verifying needs that password typed again.** Otherwise someone could register a victim's address with a password they know and wait for the victim to click a link (found by the automated security review). Later submissions never invalidate or change an earlier link. 5 wrong passwords on a link kill it. Resend copies the newest link's password, never takes one. Rejected: no password at sign-up and set it on the verify page (changes the designed form) |
| Verify, reset | Neither starts a session (the one place a session is issued stays `services.start_session`, shared with password login and Google). Reset bumps that user's `session_epoch`, checks the password's strength before touching the token, and works for a Google-only account (it adds a password). Forgot and resend send nothing for an unknown, unconfirmed (forgot only), suspended or malformed address, and answer the same 202. Login answers an unconfirmed account like a wrong password |
| Limits (`app/limits.py`, in memory like the login limiter, one `web` replica) | Sign-up: 5 per hour per address, and one mail per address per minute (a second sign-up for one address within a minute does nothing and answers 202: stops mailbox flooding). Resend and forgot: 1 per minute per email, 5 per hour per address, silent. Waitlist: same style. Wrong verify passwords: 5 per link. nginx (`deploy/nginx.conf`): one shared zone `notes_api_auth` (10 a minute per address, burst 10) for sign-up, resend, verify, forgot, reset and Google start; `notes_waitlist` (5 a minute, burst 5); the Google callback is not limited. A guard test derives the routes from `auth.PUBLIC_API`, so a public route cannot ship without a zone |
| Google (`app/google_oauth.py`, `app/google_signin.py`) | Code flow with PKCE (S256), scopes `openid email profile` only, state stored hashed in `oauth_flows`, single use, 10 minutes, **bound to the browser by a `google_flow` cookie** (against login CSRF), `return_to` checked by a regular expression (one leading slash, no scheme). **The ID token's signature is not verified**: it arrives straight from Google's token endpoint over TLS in answer to a request with our client secret and PKCE verifier, which OIDC Core 3.1.3.7 allows and Google's guidance accepts; issuer, audience, expiry, issued-at, nonce, subject and a boolean-true `email_verified` are checked. Matching order: `google_sub`; same email (verified account: link; **unverified password account: verified, linked, password cleared, links dropped**; an account linked to another Google account is never relinked); else a new verified account with no password, if the pilot has room or the address holds a live invitation, and the terms were accepted (a flag in the flow cookie set by `?accept_terms=true`). Under the same lock as sign-up. A turned-away visitor gets a 15-minute signed `pilot_email` cookie (its own key label) and `/pilot-full`; no address is ever put in a URL. The whole feature exists only when `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` are set |
| Waitlist and invitations | Public `POST /api/waitlist` answers 202 for every outcome. The admin's invite takes the sign-up lock, replaces any earlier link, reserves a place for 7 days (counted in `slots_taken`), and mails the link when a mail provider exists, else returns it once. Redeemed by password sign-up (`invite_token`, for that address only) or by Google sign-in (matched by the verified Google address, no token in the URL) |
| Admin API (`app/admin.py`, `app/api_admin.py`) | Every route depends on `require_admin` (403 `forbidden`); a test lists every `/api/admin/` route and proves 403 and 401. No route reads another user's meetings, notes or calendar. `is_admin` cannot be set through the API (strict bodies). **Last-admin guard runs under an advisory lock** (`ADMIN_LOCK = 6003`): two admins cannot remove each other at once (test widens the gap). Suspending bumps the user's `session_epoch` (reactivating does not revive old cookies). Reset link: only for verified, active, **non-admin** users, shown once, the user gets a `warning` notification, the act is logged with ids only |
| Deleting (`app/deletion.py`, `scripts/delete_user.py`) | The admin's `DELETE` marks the user `deleted` (signed out and the place free at once; the existing check constraint has no `deleting`, and a `deleted` row is already treated as absent everywhere). The worker purges every pass: stops running bots, deletes Vexa's copy and the local audio, then meetings, templates, vocabulary, settings, notifications, queued mail, tokens and the waitlist row, then the user. Vexa trouble raises before anything is deleted, so the next pass retries. `usage_entries` keep seconds and `email_hash` only. The script does the same for one address (asks first, refuses the last admin) |
| Notifications (`app/notifications.py`) | List (newest first, paged), mark read (first read time kept), read-all, `unread_notifications` on the session; a foreign id is 404 |
| Hourly sweeps (`app/maintenance.py`, run by the worker) | Expired sign-in flows, spent tokens older than 7 days, notifications older than 90 days, unverified accounts 48 hours after their last sign-up submission, the mail outbox |
| Bots (`app/services.py`) | `bot_already_active` now means the caller's own running meeting. A full note taker is `bot_busy` ("The note taker is busy right now. Try again in a few minutes.") and says nothing about whose; the limit is `MAX_ACTIVE_BOTS` (default 1, counts `bot_sent` and `processing` as before). Phase C/D replace it with per-host placement |
| Usage (`app/pipeline.py`) | The ledger row is committed before the OpenAI call and the meeting's row lock is taken again, so a failed transcription still counts its cost |
| Legal pages | The account, Google sign-in, email-use, retention (unconfirmed accounts, waitlist, the hours ledger) and pilot sentences, in the Jinja templates **and** the Next copies (a web test keeps them equal); both "Last updated" dates and `TERMS_VERSION` are 3 November 2026. "Notices about meetings" was removed from the email sentence (not sent yet) |

## Operate / roll back
Deploy order and the nginx edit are in `deploy/README.md` section 10: environment first, migration `0008` with the deploy (additive; downgrade only drops the column), the nginx blocks added **by hand** to the live site file (never copy `deploy/nginx.conf` over it), then `GET /api/pilot` shows `closed`. Open the pilot only as the admin with `PUT /api/admin/pilot` (curl recipe in the README); keep `password_signup_enabled` off until a real verification mail has been delivered once; close it again with `signups_open: false`. Delete one account with `scripts/delete_user.py`. If Resend is down, mail waits and retries; `GET /api/admin/mail-failures` lists what failed, and `POST /api/admin/users/{id}/reset-link` is the fallback for a reset.

**Migration number:** this PR adds `0008`. Phase C's plan also names its calendar migration `0008`; whichever merges second must renumber and re-point `down_revision`, or the repository has two heads (`test_single_head` fails).

## Follow-ups
- **Fix before sign-up is switched on for anyone (recorded, not fixed):** a sign-up flood can become an outage. About 20 addresses at 5 an hour keep the 100 unconfirmed-account cap full of fresh rows (everyone then gets 429), the at-cap clearing can evict a real person's pending account after an hour, and there is no global cap on outgoing mail (each sign-up, resend and forgot queues a mail). Options: a global per-hour mail budget, a per-address concurrent cap, a proof-of-work or captcha step, evicting by address rather than age.
- **Review findings handled in this PR** (automated security review of the commits): pre-registration takeover (fixed with the bound password above), mail flooding of a third party's inbox (per-address sign-up mail limit), guessing a link's password (5 per link), last-admin race (lock), suspension not ending sessions for good (epoch bump), a reset link for another admin (refused), a stranger's waitlist name in our mail (greeting only for plain names). **Acknowledged, not changed:** a rate-limited second sign-up within a minute is dropped silently, so a hostile first submission can delay the real owner by a minute at a time (they can also use Google); resend copies the newest link's password rather than asking for one, which cannot confirm anything the person did not type.
- The ID token is not signature-checked (see Google above); revisit if tokens are ever read from anywhere but the token endpoint.
- Notifications are written for `usage.hours_low` and `account.reset_link_issued` only; "could not join" and admin alerts come with Phase C.
- Mail templates are plain; the designer's `Emails.dc.html` is not applied. A mail's text is not captured in the FE guide (only the queued payload).
- No admin screen yet (the API is curl-able); `slots_taken` and the waitlist have no UI.
- Limits are in memory and per process (one `web` replica); move to the database or Redis before a second replica.
- The common-password list is still the 384-entry file; no breached-password lookup.
- A non-ASCII email is refused everywhere (`invalid_email`, or the `email_unsupported` redirect for Google) until the email is casefolded consistently.
- Run `docs/spike/edge-secret/e2e.sh` and the web e2e scripts once against a compose stack (still not run).
