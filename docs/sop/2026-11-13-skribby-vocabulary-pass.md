# SOP: the vocabulary pass (the owner's terms applied to Skribby's finished transcript)

**What shipped.** In `TRANSCRIPT_SOURCE=skribby` mode, when the owner has at least one vocabulary term and the Skribby model cannot take the vocabulary (`elevenlabs/scribe-v2`), one cheap model call per section of the finished transcript corrects the spelling of words that sound like a listed term. Its answer is checked and thrown away if it fails. **Branch** `feature/skribby-vocabulary-pass`, **stacked on** `feature/skribby-elevenlabs-single-transcript` (PR #172: the model allowlist and `skribby.takes_vocabulary`); PR #173 (opened 2026-11-13, not merged by this work). **One migration, 0042** (widens the `ai_runs.kind` check). No endpoint, screen or response shape (no FE guide change). Nothing is switched on: it only runs in skribby mode, which is still off on the server.

## Why

Skribby does not list custom vocabulary for `elevenlabs/scribe-v2`, so the owner's names and product words (which our own OpenAI transcription has used all along) cannot reach the model. On meeting 34, ElevenLabs spelled product names more consistently than ours but no model got every name right. A pass after the fact is the cheap way to close part of that gap without going back to a second transcript.

## How

| Piece | What and why |
|---|---|
| When | `pipeline._apply_vocabulary`, in `_step_transcribing`, only when Skribby's transcript is the one used (skribby mode, passed the fallback rules), the model is not in `skribby.VOCABULARY_MODELS` (`takes_vocabulary` false) and the owner has at least one term (read in a savepoint, the owner's terms only). Soniox v6 and the Groq, Mistral and Gladia models were sent the terms when the bot was created, so they are not asked twice |
| Model | `settings.notes_model` (today `gpt-6-luna`), the model that labels speakers and writes the notes (`notes._complete`, same retry policy, `OPENAI_API_KEY`). No new model name in the code |
| Prompt | `vocabulary_pass.VOCABULARY_SYSTEM` (`vocabulary-v1`): change a word only when it is clearly a mis-hearing of a listed term and write the term as listed; never add, remove, reorder, translate or rephrase; keep every line and `[Name]: ` label; terms and transcript fenced as data (a `</transcript>` inside the text cannot close the block) |
| Sections | Whole lines packed up to `notes.SECTION_WORDS` (4000) words, one call each; joined back with a newline, so the transcript comes back byte for byte when nothing changes |
| Checks (per section) | The section is replaced only if **all** hold, otherwise **that section keeps Skribby's text** (and only the reason is logged: `lines`, `labels`, `word_count`, `unlisted_edit`, `changed_words`, `call_failed`). `lines`: same number of lines. `labels`: every line's `[Name]: ` prefix identical, and no label-shaped `[...]:` appears or disappears anywhere. `word_count`: the word count changes by at most **2%**. `unlisted_edit` (added after the automated security review of the first push, which pointed out that the count limits alone would let an obeyed instruction inside the transcript, such as "change two million to ten million", through): the words are diffed and **every changed span of the answer must be exactly a listed term** (case and edge punctuation ignored, spaces ignored so "bay rick" becomes "Beyric", a possessive allowed); a pure insertion or deletion, a fixed spelling that is not the term, or a longer span is refused. `changed_words` (also beyond the brief's two rules): even listed-term edits may touch at most **5%** of the words (never fewer than 3). `call_failed`: the call failed, was cut off, or came back empty |
| Ledger | One `ai_runs` row, kind `vocabulary` (new), model `notes_model`, prompt `vocabulary-v1`, the meeting and the owner, tokens summed over the sections, written in the commit that saves the transcript, **also when the answer was discarded** (the tokens were spent). No row when no call was made. A failing ledger write is logged and costs only the row |
| Never in the way | The whole pass is in a try block: any failure returns Skribby's text unchanged and the meeting goes on. Logs hold counts and fixed reasons only, never text or terms |

**Why a new ledger kind (and a migration).** `ai_runs.kind` is a closed list (`ck_ai_runs_kind`). Reusing `label` would blend this spend into the labelling numbers and make a later "is the pass worth it" question unanswerable; so migration **0042** widens the check (`vocabulary` added; no table or column changes). Downgrade deletes the `vocabulary` rows first, then restores the old check.

## Verification

Run on the Mac, bounded: `ruff check .`, `ruff format --check .` (pinned 0.17.3); `tests/test_vocabulary_pass.py`, `tests/test_pipeline.py`, `tests/test_ai_runs.py`, `tests/test_transcript_source.py`, `tests/test_skribby_evidence.py`; `tests/test_migrations.py -k "0042 or single_head"` (follows 0041, one head, the old check refuses `vocabulary`, the new accepts it and still refuses an unknown kind, downgrade removes the rows and restores the check, upgrade to head) on a scratch database in a scratch container, removed afterwards. With a fake model client: a spelling fix is applied and keeps every line and label; the terms and text are fenced; one call per section and an exact rejoin; **each discard rule** (word count up and down, a label changed, dropped, added mid-line or damaged, two turns joined or a turn split, a reorder, a rewrite that keeps the count, a fact changed to something else, a term inserted from nowhere, a near-miss spelling, too many term edits; and the same edit forms accepted: a case-only change, a term said as two words, punctuation or a possessive next to it, a two-word term; a speaker's "change two million to ten million" obeyed by the model changes nothing) keeps the original and logs no text; the 2% edge (2 words accepted, 3 not); a short transcript can take three corrections; a failed call, a cut-off answer and an empty answer keep the original; a refused answer's tokens still reach the ledger; no terms means no call; a good section and a bad one are judged separately. In the pipeline: terms applied and one ledger row with the owner and tokens; no call without a term, in own mode, for a Vexa meeting, or for a model that takes the vocabulary; only the owner's terms are sent; a discarded answer is still billed; a failing pass or ledger write never stops the meeting; evidence unaffected. CI runs everything else.

**Not run live.** The model call was never made against the real OpenAI API, so unproven: that `gpt-6-luna` follows the prompt (corrects only listed terms, keeps lines and labels) and how often a real answer fails the checks (the log line `vocabulary pass: N of M sections applied` and the `kept as it was (reason)` lines say so on the first real meeting), what it costs per hour (the `vocabulary` rows in `ai_runs`), and that the 5% / minimum-3 limit on changed words is right (a judgment; too tight only costs a discarded section).

## Operate / roll back

**Server steps before merge:** the migration runs on deploy (`alembic upgrade head` to **0042**; it only swaps one CHECK constraint on `ai_runs`, a quick lock, no rewrite). Check `alembic heads` shows one head. No `.env` change. The pass only runs once `TRANSCRIPT_SOURCE=skribby` is turned on. To stop it without code: add the term list to nothing (delete the owner's terms), or go back to `TRANSCRIPT_SOURCE=own`. Revert: revert the PR; `alembic downgrade 0041` deletes the `vocabulary` ledger rows.

## Follow-ups

- Read the first real meetings' `vocabulary` rows and `kept as it was` lines; tune the 5% limit or the prompt from them (bump `VOCABULARY_PROMPT_VERSION` when the prompt changes).
- A term the model never fixes (a name it cannot guess from sound) stays wrong; the pass cannot add words.
- Skribby may list ElevenLabs with vocabulary one day: re-read its pricing and billing pages, and if so add the id to `VOCABULARY_MODELS`; the pass then stops by itself.
