# SOP: billing stage S10, the one-time trial and the end of the pilot hours

**What shipped.** (1) A one-time **trial**: a `trial` hour grant of `TRIAL_SECONDS` (3 hours = 10800) given once per person when their email is first confirmed (password link or Google), ending at the hours used or 14 days after it is given, whichever is first. It also closes the S2 "before enforce" gap: accounts made after migration 0049 hold no hours, and a scheduler sweep gives every such account its trial. (2) The **pilot hours' end**: with `PAID_PLANS_OPEN_ON` set, every pilot grant ends 60 days later, and each person with pilot hours left gets one mail and one bell entry 14 days before. (3) A **trial budget** (`TRIAL_DAILY_BUDGET_SECONDS`) on how many trial seconds can start in any 24 hours; `max_accounts` stays as the other brake. **Nothing is unfinished and nothing needs code to switch on**: the features are controlled by env values whose defaults are off (`TRIAL_SECONDS=0`, `PAID_PLANS_OPEN_ON` unset); with the defaults no row is written for anyone and no extra scheduler job runs. The trial is given in any `HOURS_MODE`; the pilot expiry acts only while the hours rule is in force (its mail promises what only `enforce` does). PR: [#226](https://github.com/innocent98/beyric-note-taker/pull/226), branch `feature/trial-and-pilot-expiry`. It is stage S10 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-billing-and-plans-design.md` (sections 3.8, 3.9, 8, 11). **No migration** (the migration number 0053 stays free for S9). It was proved locally end to end with the real web and scheduler processes (`scripts/local_trial_e2e.py`, 14 checks, below). FE guide: `docs/fe-integration-guide-billing.md` (section "The trial and the pilot's end"; captures in `docs/fe-captures/trial/`).

## Why

Under `HOURS_MODE=enforce` a person is allowed to record only from hours they hold. An account made after migration 0049 holds none, so every new sign-up would be refused `no_plan` (S2 SOP, "Before switching on", point 1). The trial is the way a new person gets hours without a card. The pilot hours had no end, so a pilot account that only reads would keep Ask, translation and rewriting switched on for ever (spec 3.9); Adebayo decided on 2026-11-16 that they expire 60 days after paid plans open, with one mail before.

## How, and what was decided

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| **The trial grant** (`app/trial.py`) | `give` writes a `kind='trial'` grant: `seconds = TRIAL_SECONDS`, `starts_at` = the moment it is given, `expires_at` = that plus `TRIAL_DAYS`, `email_hash` = the fingerprint of the address. Everything after that is the existing rule: spent first (spec 3.2), exactly 3 hours used gives `no_hours`, the 14th day gives `none`/`no_plan`, the 80% and 100% notices work as for any grant |
| **Once per person and per fingerprint** | No trial for an admin, an unconfirmed, suspended or deleted account, or anyone (found by user id **or** email fingerprint, `entitlements.mine`) who has ever held a `trial` **or `pilot`** grant, ended or not. A deleted account's grants keep their fingerprint (the user id is set null, `app/deletion.py`), so deleting and signing up again with the same address gives nothing; a pilot account has its own hours, even when all are used. An app-wide advisory lock (6021) serialises the check and the insert, so two workers cannot give one person two trials or spend one budget twice |
| **When it is given** | At the three places an account becomes confirmed: `signup.verify_email` (password link), `google_signin._create` (a new Google account) and `google_signin._link_by_email` (Google confirming an unconfirmed password account). It runs in that transaction and in a savepoint: a failure is logged by exception class and never fails the sign-up. The settings reach it from the two routes (`api_accounts.py`) |
| **The sweep** (`trial.sweep`, scheduler job `trial_sweep`, every minute, registered only while `TRIAL_SECONDS` > 0) | Gives a trial to every confirmed, active, non-admin account that holds no trial or pilot grant, oldest confirmation first. It is the safety net for a failed sign-up hook, the way budget-delayed people get theirs, and **the fix for the 0049 gap**: when the flag goes on, everyone who made an account after 0049 gets a trial within a minute. A late trial **starts when it is given**: the 14 days are never spent waiting |
| **Given in any mode; the backlog rule covers it** | `trial.enabled` = `TRIAL_SECONDS` > 0, whatever `HOURS_MODE` is, so a person who signs up before `enforce` already holds their 3 hours when it comes on (a first version gave it only under `enforce`; Adebayo wanted new accounts to get it whenever it is on). The catch: only `enforce` splits a recording across grants, so what a trial holder recorded before then has no allocation. The existing backlog rule now covers trial grants as well as pilot grants (`entitlements.BACKLOG_KINDS`, used by `unallocated_seconds`, `hours.reconcile_backlog` and `scripts/hours_reconcile_backlog.py`): usage counted since the grant began and never allocated comes off it, so nobody gets those hours twice. Tested (2 h recorded in shadow leaves 1 h at `enforce`, before and after settling) and proved live (below). Usage from before the trial was given never comes off it |
| **The budget** | `TRIAL_DAILY_BUDGET_SECONDS` (0 = no cap): a trial is given only if the trial seconds given in the last 24 hours (a rolling window, `starts_at > now - 24h`) plus one more still fit. Exactly the budget is allowed; a grant 24 hours old is out of the window. People past the budget hold nothing until the sweep gives them theirs. A budget smaller than one trial would stop every trial silently, so it stops the process at startup. Rolling, not a calendar day: no midnight cliff and no time-zone argument |
| **`max_accounts`** | Unchanged (`app/signup.py`): a sign-up past it never becomes an account, so it never reaches a trial. Tested with the trial on |
| **The pilot end** (`app/pilot_expiry.py`, job `pilot_expiry`, hourly, registered only while `PAID_PLANS_OPEN_ON` is set) | Unset: nothing happens. Set: every pilot grant still in force gets `expires_at` = the start of the day after `PAID_PLANS_OPEN_ON + PILOT_EXPIRY_DAYS` (60), UTC. After that instant the grant is simply not in force, so `entitlements` (state, join, gates) does the rest even if the scheduler is down. The job acts only under `HOURS_MODE=enforce` (under `off` and `shadow` the old cap decides, and the mail would describe a rule that is not on; a warning at startup says so when the date is set without it). A grant already past its own end is never brought back by moving the date |
| **The one mail** | From `PILOT_EXPIRY_NOTICE_DAYS` (14; checked at startup: at least the minimum notice, below `PILOT_EXPIRY_DAYS`, and at most 60 so the bell entry, pruned at 90 days, outlives the window) days before the end, each **confirmed**, active, non-admin person who holds pilot hours that are not used up gets one bell entry and one mail (`alerts.notify_user`, so `notify_email` and suppression are respected). Unconfirmed accounts are never written to: migration 0049 gave a pilot grant to every non-admin row, and an unconfirmed one may be someone else's address. The dedupe key names the person's end date: once per person **per date**. Moving the date later sends one corrected mail for the new date; moving it earlier never shortens a date a person was already told |
| **Minimum notice** (`PILOT_EXPIRY_MIN_NOTICE_DAYS`, 7) | A person's hours never end sooner than 7 days after their mail. If the job first acts when their end is closer (the date was set late, `enforce` came on late, the scheduler was down), their pilot grants move to the first midnight UTC at least 7 days ahead and the mail names that date. Why 7: the mail has to be read by someone who checks their inbox about once a week, and a week still leaves time to take a plan. Why move the end rather than refuse: refusing would leave them with no mail at all if the scheduler is the thing that failed; a person with nothing left to lose is not mailed and not moved |
| **Safety guard** | If the end has already passed when the date is first seen and some pilot grant in force was never given an end, nothing is expired and a warning is logged (`pilot expiry date ... has passed and some pilot hours were never given an end`): nobody loses hours unwarned. Move the date. Once every grant carries its end the job is quiet after the end (a first version warned every hour for ever) |
| **Dry run** (`scripts/trial_report.py`) | Read-only; counts and dates only: who would get a trial now, how long a budget would take to clear them, who would be mailed and from what day |

Rejected: a trial only under `enforce` (new accounts would hold nothing until the flag goes on; Adebayo asked for the opposite); an `app_state` column for the open date (it needs a migration that would collide with S8a's 0052 and S9's 0053, and Adebayo already edits `.env` for the other flags); expiring every pilot grant at one fixed instant without a mail window; a calendar-day budget (midnight cliff); a trial for admins; expiring pilot hours by a scheduled job at the end moment (the end is data on the grant, so it holds even with the scheduler down); counting `TRIAL_DAILY_BUDGET_SECONDS` on hours used rather than hours given (it would refuse a join mid-day because of strangers' use).

### The mail, word for word (**needs Adebayo's and the lawyer's confirmation**: spec 3.9, 3.7, section 9 question 10)

Title: `Your pilot hours will expire`

With `HOURS_MODE=enforce` and the feature gates not yet on:

> Paid plans opened on 1 February 2027. The recorded hours you still have from the pilot (1.5 h) can be used until the end of 2 April 2027 (UTC), and then they expire. After that the note taker will not join new meetings until you take a plan. You can still read, search and export your notes and transcripts, which we keep until you ask us to delete them or close your account. If you do not need more hours, you do not need to do anything.

With `FEATURE_GATES_MODE=enforce` as well, the second sentence continues "will not join new meetings, and Ask Beyric, new translations and rewriting notes with another template will not work, until you take a plan." The mail says only what is switched on. The dates and the hours are filled in per person (here: `PAID_PLANS_OPEN_ON=2027-02-01`, 1.5 h left; under an hour it says minutes); both texts are tested word for word in `tests/test_pilot_expiry.py`.

**What the mail does not say, on purpose:** "nothing is deleted" and "your notes stay exactly as they are" (the review found them untrue: meeting audio is deleted after 7 days, bell entries after 90, and everything goes when an account is closed). The keeping sentence is the Privacy Policy's own ("kept until you ask us to delete them or close your account", `web/components/legal/privacy-content.tsx`). **Caveat for the lawyer:** it changes what the pilot was promised ("5 hours for the pilot", the Terms line `web/components/legal/terms-content.tsx:30`), and what we say about keeping data once hours end is a lawyer question (spec 3.7). The wording, the 60 days, the 14-day notice, the 7-day minimum and the date are for Adebayo and the lawyer to confirm. **Do not set `PAID_PLANS_OPEN_ON` until they have.** The mail does not say "free", names no price, and links nowhere because no plan can be bought before S8.

## Switch-on steps (for Adebayo): env values only, one proof command per step

Everything is built and proved locally; switching on edits `.env` on the server and recreates the processes (`docker compose up -d --force-recreate web worker scheduler`; a plain restart does not re-read `.env`). Run each proof command where the app's `.env` is. This **updates the S2 "Before switching on" list** (`docs/sop/2026-11-16-hours-enforce.md`): its point 1, the sign-up gap, is closed by the trial.

**Recommended `TRIAL_SECONDS=10800` (3 hours), `TRIAL_DAYS=14`.** 3 hours is decided (spec 3.8): enough for two or three real meetings, which is what a person needs to judge the notes, at a cost of about 0.6 to 0.9 USD an hour, so about 2 to 3 USD an account (spec 3.8, 4.2); 14 days is decided so a teammate who only reads cannot keep Ask Beyric on for ever. `TRIAL_DAILY_BUDGET_SECONDS`: leave it `0` (no cap) while `max_accounts` is the brake; if you want a cost cap too, a multiple of 10800 (43200 = four new trials in any 24 hours; people past it wait and get theirs when the window frees). It must not be smaller than `TRIAL_SECONDS` (the process refuses to start).

**The rule that decides the order, in one sentence: a trial lasts 14 days in every mode, so steps 2 to 5 must happen within 14 days of each other, and the shortest safe sequence is all of them in one sitting (the shadow wait has no other purpose than reading the report).** If `HOURS_MODE=enforce` comes later than that, every trial given at step 2 has ended and those people are refused `no_plan` (no plan can be bought yet), which is exactly the sign-up gap again. Under shadow the person's session shows the new `usage` fields as null, so nobody is told their trial is running out. The preflight is in the proof of step 2 and step 5: `scripts/trial_report.py` prints `Trials in force: N, the first ends <date>` and, while `HOURS_MODE` is not `enforce`, a `WARNING` with the days left, and a second `WARNING` for trials that already ended unused. **Remedy if some already ended before `enforce`:** restart their clock with `docker compose exec -T db psql -U postgres -d notetaker -c "update hour_grants set expires_at = now() + interval '14 days' where kind = 'trial' and expires_at <= now()"` (it only touches trials, restarts them for 14 days, and the reconcile step in step 4 already treats what they recorded in shadow); to give trials that are still running 14 more days instead, use `expires_at > now()` in place of `expires_at <= now()`. Then go to step 5 straight away.

If a step's proof does not show what it must, do not go on: revert that step's `.env` line and recreate (below).

## Operate / roll back

* **Stop giving trials:** unset `TRIAL_SECONDS` (or `0`) and recreate web, worker and scheduler. Trials already given stay as data and keep working until they end; nothing is taken back.
* **Take back one trial:** a direct SQL edit of that `hour_grants` row (set its `seconds` to 0 or its `expires_at` to now); there is no screen and an admin gift only adds hours. Not built here.
* **Cancel the pilot end:** unsetting `PAID_PLANS_OPEN_ON` stops the job but **does not clear the end already written on the grants**. To clear it: `docker compose exec -T db psql -U postgres -d notetaker -c "update hour_grants set expires_at = null where kind = 'pilot' and expires_at > now()"` (only grants still in force; an ended grant stays ended). To move the date instead, set the new date: the grants are moved to it, and a corrected mail goes for the new date.
* **A person says they got no trial:** they are in `trial.waiting` if they are confirmed, active, not an admin and hold no trial or pilot grant; the sweep gives it within a minute unless the daily budget is spent (`scripts/trial_report.py` shows the free room). If they ever held a trial or a pilot grant under that address, they are not owed one.
* Revert the PR to remove the code; there is no migration to undo. Grants and notices already written stay.

| The session payload has **no field for the pilot's end** (`pilot_ends_at`); only the mail and bell carry the date | the sidebar cannot show "your pilot hours end on ..." (an additive `Status` and `UsageOut` field, which S8a is editing: left out here on purpose) |
| A trial is not stopped by anything but the grant: no IP, device or card check; a person with many email addresses gets many trials. **Aliases count as different addresses**: `ada+1@gmail.com` and `a.da@gmail.com` have different fingerprints from `ada@gmail.com` (the fingerprint is `usage.email_hash`, the lower-cased trimmed address, as the ledger and spec 3.8 define it) | the brakes are a confirmed mailbox, `max_accounts`, the budget; a new trial costs about 2 to 3 USD (spec 3.8). Merging aliases (strip `+tag`, Gmail dots) would be a product decision that also changes what the ledger treats as one person, so it is **not done here**; raised by the automated security review of this commit, 2026-11-17. If trials start being farmed, add a canonical fingerprint column and a per-origin limit |
| Pilot hours end at the end of a UTC day, and the mail says so; a person in another zone sees the date in UTC | a per-person zone is stored (`#82`); using it in the mail is a small change if wanted |
| A trial given while the person is **not** a pilot but a reader-only teammate costs nothing unless they record; they still get one | spec 3.8 gives it to everyone at first confirmed sign-in |
| `trial.waiting` loads every waiting account each minute and the sweep stops at the first refused one | cheap for hundreds of accounts; add a `LIMIT` if sign-up ever floods |
