# SOP: billing stage S8a, subscriptions, packs and the lapse states (the money half)

**What shipped.** Plans, packs and subscriptions as data and as a state machine, with our own billing clock: a person can start a hosted checkout for a plan or a pack on the route their billing country picks, a **verified** payment grants their hours, the saved card renews the plan each month, a failed renewal gets two retries (day 1 and day 2) and **3 days of grace in which no new hours are granted**, and the grace ends in a lapse. Upgrade (prorated), downgrade (at renewal), cancel (at period end) and resume exist. The person's own routes are under `/api/billing/...`. **All dark:** `BILLING_ENABLED` is off by default and the routes answer only for `BILLING_ALLOWED_EMAILS`; with Adebayo's Paystack TEST keys an allow-listed account can run a real test-mode purchase (runbook below, **never run**). PR: [#227](https://github.com/innocent98/beyric-note-taker/pull/227), branch `feature/subscriptions-and-lapse`. Stage S8 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-billing-and-plans-design.md` (sections 2, 3, 5, 7, 8). **S8a is the money half; the billing screens are S8b, a second PR.** FE guide: `docs/fe-integration-guide-billing.md`, section "Subscriptions, packs and the lapse" (captures in `docs/fe-captures/billing-subscriptions/`, FakeProvider path).

## Why

Stages S1 to S7 built the hours, the gates, the provider layer, the billing country and two adapters, all dark, and nobody could buy anything. S8a is the first stage where money turns into hours. The rules it must keep are the ones Adebayo decided: hours come only from a payment that was **re-read from the provider**; a failed renewal never grants a new period (so the worst case in the grace is zero hours beyond what was already paid for); a person is never charged after they cancel; and two workers, a retry or a webhook replay can never make two periods out of one payment.

## How, and what was decided

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| **One status machine, one table** (`app/billing/states.py`) | Five statuses (`active`, `past_due`, `lapsed`, `canceled`, `ended`) and a table of (status, event) to (status, effects). A pair not in the table is refused. Pure functions, no database: `due_action` (what the clock asks), `effective_status` (what a subscription is by the clock when the scheduler has not run), `apply` (what an event does). Only a `payment_succeeded` row carries the `start_period` effect: nothing else can ever grant a period (a test proves it by iterating the table) |
| **The timeline** | Due moment `D` = the period's end. Attempt 0 at `D` charges the saved card. A failure opens the grace (`D + 72 h`), sends the day-0 notice and schedules attempt 1 at `D + 24 h`; a failure there schedules attempt 2 at `D + 48 h`; a failure there sends the last-day notice and stops. At `D + 72 h` the subscription is `lapsed`. A payment that succeeds at any of those moments, or later, makes it `active` again with a NEW period starting at the payment date (or at `D` when paid within an hour of it, so an on-time renewal never drifts) |
| **`canceled` vs `lapsed` vs `ended`** | `lapsed`: the grace ended unpaid. `canceled`: the person stopped it and the period (or the grace) ended. `ended`: closed otherwise (a deleted account). Cancelling in the grace stops the retries and the grace's end is a cancel, not a lapse |
| **Where the clock runs** (`app/billing/renewals.py`, registered in `app/scheduler.py`) | A job of the **leader-elected scheduler process**, on its sync loop, every 60 seconds, only while `BILLING_ENABLED` is on. **Rejected: the worker**: it can sit for minutes in one transcription pass, and a renewal must not wait behind that. **Rejected: the dispatch loop**: a provider's slow answer must never delay a join |
| **One subscription per transaction, under its row lock** | `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, so two schedulers never take the same subscription |
| **An attempt is a row we made first** | The attempt payment (kind `renewal`, `attempt` 0 to 2, `cycle_end` = the period's end) is created and committed before the provider is asked; the unique index on (subscription, cycle_end, attempt) lets exactly one worker hold a slot. The charge is called with no lock held |
| **A charge answer grants nothing** | `charge_saved_method` answering `successful` is re-read with `fetch_transaction` and judged by `settle.settle_transaction`, the S4 rules a webhook gets (our reference, our currency, at least our amount). Only then does the grant handler run. If the provider cannot confirm at that moment the attempt is **not** counted as failed (money may have moved): the payment stays pending and the next pass settles it by reference |
| **An unknown charge outcome is resolved, never repeated** | A timeout or a lost answer leaves the payment `pending`. Before any new charge in that period the pass asks the provider for it by OUR reference (`provider.lookup_by_reference`: Paystack `verify_reference`, Flutterwave `fetch_by_reference`). While nothing is known the subscription waits (the clock for the grace and the lapse keeps running); after six hours the admins get one alert |
| **A 3-D Secure charge keeps its own link** | A saved-card charge the bank wants the payer to confirm comes back `pending` with the bank's link, stored as the payment's `pay_url`. `POST /subscription/pay-link` first **asks the provider, by our reference, about every unpaid renewal payment of the period** (newest first): `successful` is settled (and the call answers 409 `nothing_due`), `failed` is marked failed, a charge still open keeps its OWN link **however old it is**, a charge with no page yet (queued) answers 409 `payment_in_progress`, and a provider that cannot be asked is 502 `provider_error` (only a pay-link checkout under a day old is reused unchecked, being only a page). A new page exists only when the provider says every open one failed. The next retry does not charge again while a charge waits. (Review fix: the first version reused the link for a day only, then made a second checkout without asking.) |
| **The pay link otherwise** | A fresh hosted checkout for the renewal amount, on the route the person is on now |
| **The grace and the person's hours** | Entering the grace extends the period's plan grants to the grace's end (they ended at `D`): the old period's leftover stays usable for 3 days and **nothing new is granted**. A payment ends them at that moment (no rollover); the lapse leaves them ended. One grant per payment (`unique (kind, source payment)`), so a replay cannot grant twice |
| **`entitlements` learns the states** (`app/entitlements.py`, writer: this stage) | `status()` reads the person's subscription by the clock: `past_due` wins over everything (gates open, only the hours still left), `lapsed` shows only when nothing better (a pilot grant with hours) does. The hours still come only from grants. `app/placement.py` needed no change: it already asks `entitlements.check_join` |
| **Checkout** (`subscriptions.py`) | Plan: the route the billing country picks (`routing.provider_for`), the price row valid now in that currency, a pending payment committed, then the provider's hosted page. Pack: needs a live plan (a plan in its grace counts), paid on the route the plan was SOLD on. Upgrade: the same, for the prorated difference. Nothing is ever granted by a checkout call or by the page the payer returns to |
| **Saved card** | `settle.TRANSACTION_HANDLERS` (new, runs just before the grant handlers, same transaction) saves the reusable token from a verified `plan`, `renewal` or `upgrade` payment, encrypted with the vault (new purpose `payment_method`), with the email the provider saw (Paystack needs it). One card per person and provider; a pack does not replace it. A card that cannot be saved (no vault key) is logged by type and told to the admins, **never allowed to stop the hours that were paid for**; the renewal then uses the pay link |
| **Duplicate and late money** | A payment whose period was already settled (`cycle_end` is not the subscription's `current_period_end`), a second plan payment for a person who already has a live plan, or money for a cancelled or ended subscription grants nothing and raises one admin alert naming the payment (ids only): the money arrived, a human refunds it (refunds are S9) |
| **Upgrade** | Not in the grace. Amount = `(new - old) * days left / period`, rounded down to a minor unit; extra hours = `(new hours - old hours) * days left / period`, rounded down to 0.1 h. The plan changes and the extra hours are granted when the payment is verified; an upgrade must land INSIDE the period it was for, judged by the clock at the payment: paid at or after the due moment, before the scheduler pass has run or while the renewal's outcome is unknown, it is a duplicate (refund alert, no plan change, no grant). A webhook processed after the due moment is judged at that moment. **Rejected: charging the saved card for an upgrade** (a hosted checkout is simpler and can carry a new card; the screens can offer both later) |
| **Downgrade** | Stored as `pending_plan_code`, applied by the renewal at the new plan's price. Choosing the current plan takes it back. Not in the grace |
| **Price change** | The subscription keeps the row it was sold at; the renewal asks for the current row (`plans.renewal_price`) |
| **Country change that crossed routes** | The saved card belongs to the old provider and cannot move: at the renewal no charge is made (a recorded failed attempt), the pay link is a fresh checkout on the NEW route and price list, and paying it moves the subscription's provider and currency. `profile.live_subscription` (the S5 stub, now real) answers with the route the subscription was SOLD on, so crossing is judged on that, never on today's table. `settle_due_country` writes a due pending change before a renewal or a pay link |
| **Mail** | Day-0, last-day, lapse, ended and receipt notices are a bell entry plus a **plain alert mail without a link** (the alert template never carries one) that tells the person to open Billing, which has a pay button (`can_pay_now`). **A mail with the pay link inside is not built** (see follow-ups) |
| **Account deletion** | `admin.schedule_deletion` ends the live subscription (no refund of the paid period, spec 3.7) and deletes the saved cards |
| **Seeded data** | `plan_20h` (20 hours, `is_public` false, so nothing prints it on /pricing) at **18.00 USD** and **NGN 25,000.00** (2,500,000 kobo; decided 2026-11-17); `pack_10h` (10 hours, valid 12 months) at 10.00 USD ("about 10 USD" is seeded as exactly 10.00: confirm) and at **NGN 14,000.00 (1,400,000 kobo), which is only a PROPOSAL and is seeded INACTIVE** (`products.active = false`: not listed, not sold; the SOP's switch-on section has the one statement that activates it). **Not seeded, Adebayo's open facts:** the second tier and the plan names ("20 hours a month" is a working name) |

Departures from the spec, on purpose: **`pending_billing_country` stays on the billing profile** where S5 put it (the spec's `subscriptions` column list has it); no second copy. **`subscriptions` has no `payment_method_id`**: the card is found by (person, provider), so a new card from any later payment simply takes over. **No `pending` subscription status**: a subscription exists from its first verified payment; what is being bought is on the payment row (`plan_price_id`, `product_id`).

## Operate / roll back

**Turn it on for a test account (test mode, no real money).** Preconditions: the server `.env` has the Paystack TEST keys and `PAYSTACK_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `BILLING_LIVE=false` (the default), `TOKEN_ENC_KEY` set (it is, for the calendar), `.env`: `BILLING_ENABLED=true`, `BILLING_ALLOWED_EMAILS=<the test account's email>`. Then run migrations (`alembic upgrade head` gives 0052) and recreate web, worker **and scheduler** (the billing clock lives in the scheduler).

1. **Check the naira price is there:** the migration seeds the plan at NGN 25,000.00 (2,500,000 kobo); `preflight 1` prints `[ok] a price in NGN`. Nothing to insert. (The naira pack is inactive until Adebayo confirms its price: see the switch-on section.)
2. In the Paystack TEST dashboard set the webhook address to `https://notes.beyrictech.com/api/billing/webhooks/paystack` (**not verified live**). Without it, the sweeper settles the payment by asking Paystack within about three minutes, so the test still finishes.
3. Choose the billing country: signed in as the test account, `PUT /api/billing/profile/country` with `{"billing_country": "NG"}` (the S5 route), or from the billing profile screen when it exists. The script's `checkout` refuses with `billing_country_missing` until a country is chosen.
4. Start the checkout: `docker compose exec -T web python scripts/billing_test_purchase.py checkout test@account.example` prints the Paystack link and our reference. Open it and pay with a Paystack test card from the spec (section 5.5: `4084 0840 8408 4081`, any future expiry, CVV `408`; Paystack may ask for its test PIN or OTP: **not verified live**).
5. Wait for the webhook, or up to about three minutes for the sweeper.
6. **The single proof command:**
   `docker compose exec -T web python scripts/billing_test_purchase.py proof test@account.example`
   It must print `PROOF: OK` and exit 0: the plan payment is `succeeded`, one live `active` subscription is on the plan that payment bought, exactly one plan-period grant came from it with the plan's hours and the period's end, and it says whether the card is saved. Anything else prints `[FAIL]` lines and `PROOF: FAILED`.
7. Optional, the saved-card renewal: `docker compose exec -T web python scripts/billing_test_purchase.py make-due test@account.example --yes` moves the period back an hour; within 60 seconds the scheduler charges the saved test card; run `proof` again (it lists the renewal payment). Paystack's declined card `4084 0800 0000 5408` on a first purchase, then the failed-renewal timeline, is the grace test.

**Roll back.** Set `BILLING_ENABLED=false` and recreate web and scheduler: every billing route is a 404 again and the clock stops. Existing subscriptions, payments and grants stay as they are (a person who already has hours keeps them; nothing renews while it is off, and the dates then catch up on the first pass after it is turned on, charging once). The migration's downgrade refuses while any subscription or purchased-payment row exists: export them, then delete by hand if you really must.
