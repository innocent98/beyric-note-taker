# SOP: the join runner, calendar titles and the mail loop (Phase C, task C12)

## What shipped
`app/join_runner.py`: the scheduler job that sends the bot to calendar meetings (`run_due_joins`, registered by `register_join_runner` on the **dispatch** loop every tick). Alongside it: the meeting now carries its **calendar title** everywhere (`app/titles.py`), the scheduler gained a third loop for **mail** so a slow provider can never delay a join, and a calendar join becomes `completed` in the same transaction as its meeting leaving `bot_sent`. Branch `feature/c12-join-runner`, one PR (number in the PR list). **Auto-join is ON by default**; `AUTOJOIN_ENABLED=false` is the operator's emergency stop. **Security-relevant (attacker-controlled title text, row and placement locking, a bot sent without a person pressing a button): wants the phase Opus review before merge.**

## Why
Everything before it (C8 rows, C10 scheduler, C11 `place_bot`, C11b alerts) only prepared data: nothing sent a bot from a calendar. A calendar join cannot ask a person to press the button again, so every refusal needs a decision, a notice and a deadline, and the one hard rule is that **a queued meeting is an unrecorded meeting**: when the host is full the owner is told at once, the runner keeps trying every 15 s, and at the deadline the row ends as "not recorded".

## How
| Piece | Decision |
|---|---|
| When a row is due | `run_at <= now` (start minus `AUTOJOIN_LEAD_SECONDS`) in states `scheduled`, `dispatching`, `no_capacity`, and `skipped` for a reason that can clear (`hours_cap`, `user_limit`; the two "a bot is already in that meeting" reasons are final, see the review section). A row whose time has passed is due at once: a scheduler down from 09:55 to 10:05 sends the 10:00 bot at 10:05 (late join); nothing is skipped before the deadline (start + 15 min). At most 25 rows and 45 s of real time per run |
| The 2-minute re-check | Once per join (`rechecked_at`), when `starts_at - 120 s` has come: `calendar.get_event` and the result goes through **`apply_batch`**, the code the sync uses, so a move, a cancel, a decline and a rule change are applied by one implementation. A source that cannot be asked (transient error, revoked) does not hold the join back: it is asked again each tick until `run_at`, then the bot goes on what is stored. No source (provider mismatch) skips the re-check |
| Nothing is sent that the owner no longer wants | Under the row lock, immediately before the send, `_gate` re-reads: the account (suspended gives `skipped/account_suspended`), the connection through `connection_state()` (`disconnected` gives `cancelled/disconnected`, `needs_reconnect` gives `skipped/needs_reconnect`; `connected` and `degraded` go on), and the rule through C7's `will_join` over the stored event, the owner's rule and the per-meeting override (`cancelled/<reason>`: `rule_none`, `override_off`, `declined`, `not_organizer`, ...). `ended` is treated as past the deadline |
| One bot per meeting | The row is read `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` with its due conditions repeated in the same statement, so two runners cannot both take a row and a row another process already moved is not touched; `place_bot()`'s placement lock and one-bot-per-code rule are the second line (two rows, one meeting). **The row lock is held from the final check through the send to the commit of the meeting row**, the order every path uses (row, then placement lock, `wait_seconds=0` so the polling rollback cannot drop the row lock). The sync, the settings and a disconnect all take the same row lock, so one landing during a send waits for it (at most `SEND_DEADLINE_SECONDS`, 30 s) instead of slipping in; a bot already sent follows the meeting (spec 7.7) |
| `place_bot` extended | `PlacementRequest.title` (cleaned by `titles.event_title`, stored as `Meeting.title`), `wait_seconds` pass-through, and **`on_placed(session, meeting)`**: runs after the meeting row is flushed and before the commit, so the join is marked `joined` with its `meeting_id` **in the same transaction**; a failure there stops the bot like a failed flush. |
| After a refusal | `place_bot` rolls back (the row lock goes with it), so the runner takes the row again **and only writes if it is still due**: a cancel or move that landed in the gap stands (tests `test_a_cancel_that_lands_while_a_refusal_is_written_is_not_overwritten`). Every column the runner changes is set before any query, and `updated_at` is flagged modified so the stamp is the runner's clock (an unchanged attribute is left out of the UPDATE and `onupdate=now()` would overwrite it) |
| Refusal mapping | `bot_busy` (host full): `no_capacity`, notice to the owner (bell and mail, once per 30-minute bucket), admin alert, retry no sooner than 15 s later. `bot_busy` with `slot_locked` (another send is in flight): nothing written, no alarm, next tick. `hours_cap_reached`: `skipped/hours_cap` plus one notice. `user_limit`: `skipped/user_limit` plus one notice, and `failed/not_recorded` at the deadline. `already_in_meeting` (the owner's own bot is there): `skipped/duplicate_meeting`, silent. `meeting_has_note_taker`: `skipped/meeting_has_note_taker` plus one plain notice, **nobody's bot is stopped or adopted** (decision 4, the first placement wins; sharing waits for W3/W6). A clear send failure (`VexaError`): the row stays, retried every 15 s, one owner notice and one admin alert per bucket. An uncertain send is handled inside `place_bot` (placeholder meeting, the worker settles it). `ValueError` (no template) and an unsupported link: `failed` with the reason, once |
| At the deadline | `failed`, `skip_reason=not_recorded`, `last_error` "Not recorded: the note taker could not join before the 15 minutes were up.", an owner notice and email, an admin alert. A skip whose cause was already told (`hours_cap`, `duplicate_meeting`, `meeting_has_note_taker`) stays `skipped`. A row that missed its deadline over an hour ago (the runner was switched off, or the scheduler was down for hours) is closed **without** a notice: the news is old |
| Alerts | Through `app/alerts.py`; kinds start `calendar.` (a disconnect deletes them) or `admin.`; user keys per join (per 30-minute bucket where it repeats), admin keys per bucket. **Admin text names the user's address and the time, never the meeting or its link** (`alerts` also strips web addresses). The event name in any alert is `titles.alert_name`: cleaned, 80 characters, web addresses replaced by "[link removed]", bare domains and addresses written `evil[.]example[.]test` |
| `joined` to `completed` | A `before_flush` listener on `Session` in `app/models.py`: when a `Meeting`'s status is changed to anything but `bot_sent`, its `joined` join becomes `completed` in the same flush, so the worker, Cancel, End, a purge and every future path agree, and a rollback undoes both. The plan named `pipeline.py`; the listener sits at the one place every status change passes. It frees the meeting code for the next week's event |
| Connection state | `connection_state(session, join)` is the one function C13 replaces. It reads the connection's `status` column today (not a stub that always says connected: with a stub the disconnect and suspended tests could not mean anything) |
| The kill switch | `AUTOJOIN_ENABLED` (default **true**): when false `register_join_runner` registers nothing and `register_jobs` logs a warning at start. Rows then wait; the calendar sync, notices and everything else are unchanged. **Not a user setting and not off by default** (decision 2026-11-04: users connect and leave) |
| Mail off the dispatch tick (C10b follow-up) | The scheduler has a third loop, `mail`, in its own thread with its own session, every `SCHEDULER_DISPATCH_SECONDS`; `mail_outbox_send` is registered on it, and `mail.deliver_pending(..., budget_seconds=60)` stops starting mails after 60 s of real time so one pass is bounded. Proven by `test_slow_mail_provider_does_not_delay_a_dispatch_tick` (a blocked mail job, a dispatch tick that still runs its job and writes its heartbeat). The worker still has its own copy of the sender (unchanged) |
| Meeting titles | `titles.clean_title` (one plain line, 200 characters, no control or bidi characters, ZWJ kept for emoji), `meeting_name` (title or "Google Meet/Zoom <code>"), `alert_name`/`mail_title` (mail and alert text), `event_title` (what is stored). Used by `services.meeting_title` (API list and detail, export title and file name, Decisions and Review groups through `api_workspace`), `mail_templates.meeting_name` (notes-ready subject and body), the legacy Jinja pages (a `meeting_title` global) and every alert. HTML is escaped by Jinja, the export renderer and the mail layout as before; the cleaner is defence in depth plus the part that markup escaping does not cover (a bidi override, a header line break). The API answer keeps markup and addresses as characters: the front end renders text (guide) |
| `_resend` (C11 SOP follow-up) | **Proved, not changed.** A meeting waiting for its second bot (a Meet refusal pause, a Zoom block) is still `bot_sent`, so it keeps counting for the host's capacity, the user's limit and its meeting code: no other placement can interleave on that code or take that slot, and the resend adds no row and changes no count. Tests: `test_a_meeting_waiting_for_its_second_bot_still_holds_its_slot_and_its_code`, `test_resend_after_a_refusal_changes_no_count_and_makes_no_second_row`, `test_resend_into_a_meeting_another_bot_took_fails_the_meeting_and_sends_nothing_more`. What it does skip is the hours gate (the first send passed it; the resend is minutes later and recorded hours only count once audio exists) and the placement lock (it changes no count, so there is nothing to serialise). Rejected: routing it through `reserve_slot` (its own `bot_sent` row would count against itself, so it would need an "exclude this meeting" parameter; worth building only if the capacity rule ever stops counting `bot_sent`) |

## Operate / roll back
- **Server steps before merge: none required.** `AUTOJOIN_LEAD_SECONDS` (60) and `AUTOJOIN_ENABLED` (true) have safe defaults.
- **First deploy (read this; changed after the Opus review: no hand-run SQL).** The first time the runner leads (`app_state.autojoin_started_at` unset, written once), it closes **silently** (no notice, no mail, no alert; `cancelled`, reason `before_runner`) every join whose `run_at` is before that moment. So a meeting that is live at deploy time, or started up to 15 minutes before it, is **not joined retroactively**, and nobody gets a "Meeting not recorded" for a meeting that happened before the feature existed. Only joins due after the first leading tick dispatch (everything at its start minus 60 s). Migration `0015` adds the two marker columns (`app_state.autojoin_started_at`, `autojoin_paused_since`; nullable, additive). To see what to expect, read-only: `select j.id, j.state, j.platform, j.starts_at, j.run_at, left(e.title, 60) from scheduled_joins j left join calendar_events e on e.id = j.calendar_event_id where j.state = 'scheduled' and j.starts_at between now() and now() + interval '7 days' order by j.starts_at;` (through `ssh haven-respite` with the handoff's `psql` pattern; every row with `run_at` before the deploy will be closed, the rest will dispatch).
- **Stop all calendar joins without a deploy:** set `AUTOJOIN_ENABLED=false` in the server `.env`, `docker compose up -d scheduler`; the log says `AUTOJOIN_ENABLED is false`. Rows wait. The scheduler writes `app_state.autojoin_paused_since` at start while it is off; when it is turned back on the first run closes, silently (`before_runner`), every join whose 15 minutes passed meanwhile, and joins still inside their 15 minutes dispatch. The API says so: `GET /api/calendar` has `auto_join_paused`, and the Settings card and the Upcoming list show one plain line. To stop all new bots of every kind: `BOT_MAX_LOCAL=0`.
- Logs: `docker compose logs --tail 50 scheduler`: `join N could not be handled (Type)`, `sending the bot for join N failed (VexaError)`, `re-check of join N failed (Type)`, never a title, link or passcode.
- Roll back: revert the PR; no data change. A `joined` row stays `joined` (its meeting is the truth) and any `no_capacity` row waits for a runner that no longer exists, ending nothing; delete or cancel such rows by hand if needed. Meetings keep the title they were given.

## Rulings I made (the plan or brief was silent or conflicted)
| Ruling | Why | Cost if wrong |
|---|---|---|
| The runner holds the row lock through the send and does not commit a `dispatching` state first (`dispatching` rows are still handled, as due) | A committed `dispatching` needs stale-row recovery after a crash; the held lock gives the same exclusion and the "no bot after the switch" ordering for free | A switch/disconnect request waits up to 30 s behind a slow send |
| `connection_state` reads the real status instead of always saying "connected" | The disconnect and suspended tests mean nothing against a stub; C13 replaces the body either way | None |
| `hours_cap` stays `skipped` at the deadline; `user_limit` and everything else end `failed/not_recorded` (`duplicate_meeting` and `meeting_has_note_taker` are final at once, see the review section) | The first three were already told or someone is recording; "not recorded" mail for each of an out-of-hours user's meetings would be noise | A user out of hours sees `skipped`, not `failed`, in the future Upcoming list |
| Rows more than an hour past their deadline are closed without a notice | After a switch-off or long outage, a mail per old meeting is confusing | An owner is not told about a meeting missed over an hour before the runner noticed |
| Retries are "no sooner than 15 s" with the 10 s tick, so effectively 20 s | The plan says 15 s; the tick is 10 s (`SCHEDULER_DISPATCH_SECONDS`) | A try 5 s later than the plan's number |
| `_resend` proved, not rewired | See the table above | If capacity ever stops counting `bot_sent`, `_resend` needs the placement path |
| One `GoogleSource` (one token cache) shared by the sync and the runner, built in `register_jobs` | The plan's "one source per process" | None |
| The runner is registered only when `CALENDAR_ENABLED` and `AUTOJOIN_ENABLED` are both true | No calendar, no rows; switching the calendar off should stop its joins too | Turning `CALENDAR_ENABLED` off also stops joins already scheduled |

## Follow-ups
- **C13** replaces `join_runner.connection_state` (and `revive_on_reconnect` must re-run rows the runner skipped as `needs_reconnect`, as its plan says); C14's mails use the same `alert` template.
- **FE guide `fe-integration-guide-calendar-settings.md`:** its "Will join is a promise" paragraph is updated here (it said nothing sent a bot yet).
- **C15a** should show the join states and reasons with server-written words (`calendar_text`), including "Not recorded", "Waiting for room" and "A note taker is already there"; the runner's `skip_reason`/`last_error` vocabulary is in this SOP and `join_runner.py`.
- **Capacity counting:** `processing` still holds the slot (C11 follow-up, unchanged): a calendar join can hear `no_capacity` while a long transcription runs. Decide with D9a.
- **Circuit breaker for refusals per egress address** (checklist): unblocked by `notify_admins` but not built.
- **The worker still sends mail too** (C10b follow-up) and two mail senders can overshoot `MAIL_DAILY_CAP` by one batch.
- `meeting_has_note_taker`'s "sharing" copy points at W3/W6, which do not exist; the words promise nothing.
- A real Google `events.get` capture and a real Vexa duplicate-bot answer (409 assumed) are still open.
- **Hours gate not checked on `_resend`** (the SOP's choice, kept; review item 10): a user who crosses their cap during the minute between a Meet refusal and the second bot still gets that second bot. The first send passed the gate and recorded hours count only once audio exists, so the overshoot is at most one retry of one meeting. If it ever matters, route `_resend` through `reserve_slot` with an "exclude this meeting" parameter.
- **Designer gap:** the designer's files have no "auto-join is paused" state. The line is an `Alert kind="notice"` (the amber one the page already uses) between the provider row and the rules on Settings, and above the Upcoming list on Meetings (`docs/ui/parity/after/app-{settings,meetings}-auto-join-paused-1440.jpg`, the built app against the mock API); the designer to accept or redraw it.
- **Captures predating the field:** only `docs/fe-captures/calendar-state/22-state-auto-join-paused.json` has `auto_join_paused` / `auto_join_paused_text`; the older captures were not regenerated (the web tests read their timestamps). Every `GET /api/calendar` body now carries the two fields.
- **The 25-row batch** is no longer a starvation risk (rows waiting out their retry gap are left out of the query), but a run still handles at most 25 rows and 45 s; a backlog after a long outage drains over several ticks.
